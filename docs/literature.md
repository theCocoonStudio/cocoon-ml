## 1. Edelman, Edelman, Goel, Malach, Tsilivis (2024), "The Evolution of Statistical Induction Heads: In-Context Learning Markov Chains", arXiv 2402.11004 (v1, 2024-02)

**Read in full 2026-10-11 (the TeX source, research session); verified against the paper; the memory version's errors listed below.** Izzy's reference ("the paper describing circuits with Bayesian math", 2026-10-10 23:1x).

**Their setting (§2.1, App. B).** Each training sequence is drawn from its own Markov chain on k states (k = 2 or 3 in the main experiments), every row of the transition matrix drawn from Dir(α) with α = (1, ..., 1), the uniform prior on the simplex; x_1 from the chain's stationary distribution; sequence length t = 100; the loss the next-token cross-entropy averaged over positions. **Their model (§2.3):** attention-only transformers, two layers, causal mask, relative position embeddings, hidden dimension 16, AdamW at 3e-5, batch 64. **The two strategies (§2.2):** *unigram*, count the states seen so far and predict from the posterior over the stationary distribution (no closed form for finite k); *bigram*, count the transitions out of the current state and predict (c_ij + 1)/(N_i + k), the Dirichlet posterior mean, which is the Bayes-optimal predictor up to the first token.

**Their claims, as the paper states them.**
- (a) A two-layer attention-only transformer represents the bigram solution exactly (Prop. 2.1): layer one attends to the previous token and copies it into the stream; layer two at position i attends to every earlier position whose previous token equals x_i and copies the token found there; the readout is the count. Trained models converge near the Bayes-optimal loss with the attention patterns of that construction (§3.1): the *statistical induction head*.
- (b) Training passes through **three** stages: uniform predictions, then a plateau at the in-context unigram predictor, then a rapid drop to the bigram solution; the KL divergence between the model's prediction and each strategy, plotted over training, shows the handover (§3.1, their Fig. 1 right).
- (c) A one-layer transformer, with or without MLPs, with absolute or relative positions, never undergoes the transition and never reaches the solution, even trained eight times longer (App. D, their Fig. 12).
- (d) The unigram solution delays learning: on a prior where the unigram statistics carry no signal (a = b in the two-state parameterisation) the model skips the plateau and converges much faster, even when evaluated on the original distribution (§3.1, their Fig. 4; Cor. A.2: the cubic term of the gradient vanishes).
- (e) The mechanism, shown in a minimal linear model f(E) = mask(E W_k (M E)^T) E, W_k standing for the second layer and the relative-position vector v for the first: after one SGD step from a constant initialisation the second layer's gradient has a diagonal bias (B ≈ 4A, Lemma 3.1) while v's gradient carries nothing about the look-back-one coordinate and favours v_1 with a quadratic profile; a uniform W_k with v_1 dominant *is* the unigram solution; v_2 overtakes only at the second step, once W_k is aligned. "Alignment of layers": the first layer's learning signal is tied to how far the second layer has come. A two-step curriculum with η_1 = O(1/t^2), then η_2 = O(1/t) with weight decay, reaches the bigram solution (Prop. 3.3). The even coordinates of v grow by a geometric series in the chain's second eigenvalue, an even/odd pattern they also find in the transformers' positional attention just before the second drop.
- (f) Three-grams with three heads per layer show three plateaus (unigram, bigram, trigram); single-headed models cannot beat bigrams there (§3.3).

**What the memory entry had wrong.** Two stages, not three (the uniform stage was missing). "A saddle or a slower direction": the paper's account is the layers' alignment through the structure of the first-step gradient, not a saddle. The Bayes-optimal reference is closed-form for the bigram arm only; their unigram reference has no closed form. The circuit attends to the positions *after* the earlier matches, not to the matches (the same information; our induction score, `cocoonml/circuits.py`, reads the position after the previous occurrence, which is theirs).

**What it predicts for our data, corrected.** Their three stages are three predictors: the uniform (no information), the in-context unigram (the window's own state frequencies so far), the in-context bigram (the window's transitions). Our *marginal level* (decisions 54, `reference.token_counts`) is the training corpus's token frequencies, a global predictor: under their symmetric prior that is their **first** stage, not the unigram plateau. So our cells' plateau at the marginal level maps to their uniform stage, and their unigram plateau is a predictor our references do not carry at all. Repair, an instrument: the in-context unigram predictor as a **third reference** beside the marginal level and the floor, the smoothed counts of the window so far ((c_i + 1)/(n + k) in the Markov world; the window's form counts in the schema world), in `reference.py`, `runs/reference7.py` and `runs/sweep8.py`, so every trajectory is placed against all three and "does our model pass through their unigram stage" becomes a reading. Their KL-to-strategy curves are a reader we lack: the KL of the model's predictive from each reference, per window, over training, cheap beside the loss. Their (c) is what the one-layer control 07g lodges (entry 3). Their (d) bears on the ensemble: a corpus whose marginal carries signal lengthens the plateau; the schema world's marginal is non-uniform by construction (unequal shares), so a long plateau is expected there, and a corpus with the marginal flattened is the cheap test of (d) on our side.

**Our replication.** Sweep 08's two cells (decisions 55: the Markov world, 4 states, Dir(1), resolution 8, size (96, 7), rate 0.05, cap 2488; stationary, then drift at radius 1) ran 2026-10-11 05:03 to about 05:30; their lines are unread until round one. Differences from theirs to hold while reading: k = 4 against their 2 or 3 (their appendix: with more symbols the unigram phase needs longer sequences to be visible, 200 for them); our window length; our position handling (to state from `array_attention.py` when read); our loop (plain descent at one rate; theirs AdamW 3e-5); evaluation over all positions (theirs at the last token). **Status:** paper verified; replication run, unread; the third reference proposed, not built.

**The theory question it raises (for Izzy).** Whether our in-context optimum is computable: closed-form under their generator; computable under ours by the exact path with no steps and by a particle filter with steps (decisions 54, built on 2026-10-10 23:4x); and even in their model one reference, the unigram-optimal, has no closed form.

## 2. Olsson, Elhage, Nanda et al. (2022), "In-context learning and induction heads"

**Their claim**, from memory: induction heads, attention heads that find the previous occurrence of the current token and copy what followed, form during training in a phase change, and that phase change coincides with the sharp improvement of the model's in-context learning score (the loss at late positions against early ones); the evidence is correlational across model sizes and causal in small attention-only models. Their *induction score* (attention from a token to the position after its previous occurrence) and *previous-token score* are the two numbers the circuits reader computes. **For our data:** the onset should coincide with the induction score rising on the best copy. **Replication:** the two scores per copy on the held windows; their phase change against our onset step. **Status:** reader written (`cocoonml/circuits.py`), not yet run on a record.

## 3. Elhage, Nanda, Olsson et al. (2021), "A mathematical framework for transformer circuits"

**Their claim**, from memory: attention-only transformers decompose into paths through the residual stream; a two-layer model's induction circuit arises by composition between a layer-one previous-token head and a layer-two head (K-composition); one-layer attention-only models are bigram and skip-trigram statistics and cannot do induction. **For our data:** our model is attention-only with layers in a residual stream (`cocoonml/array_attention.py`), the same family, so their decomposition applies as written; a one-layer cell should not show an onset of the induction kind. **Replication:** a one-layer cell at the smallest size as the control for the circuits reader: `sweep7.py sweep-07g-r1-w96-l1-lr0.05 4 3 4 96 1 64 2 1 1 0.5 42000 0,1,2,3,free 2000 2488 1 0.05 3 300 3/4 cap <copies>`, the schema's world at (96, 1), below the derived floor on purpose (the control, not a cell of the row), the same rate, cap and ensemble as 07e's (96, 7) cells at radius 1; what it must show for their decomposition to apply: an induction score that does not rise on its best copy, and a held loss that stays nearer the marginal level than the seven-layer cell's. ## 4. Xie, Raghunathan, Liang, Ma (2022), "An Explanation of In-context Learning as Implicit Bayesian Inference", arXiv 2111.02080 (ICLR 2022)

**Read in full 2026-10-11 (the TeX source); verified; the memory entry's outline holds and gains the placement below.** **Their setting (§2):** pretraining documents drawn by first sampling a latent concept θ from a prior over a family Θ, then a sequence from the hidden Markov model whose hidden-state transitions θ parameterises; a prompt is n independent examples (an input of k−1 tokens then an output token, a delimiter between examples) all under one prompt concept θ*, followed by a test input; the in-context predictor is the argmax of the pretraining distribution's posterior predictive given the prompt, and the question is whether it recovers the prompt concept although the prompt (independent examples concatenated) is a low-probability sequence under pretraining.

**Their claims.** (a) The posterior predictive marginalises over concepts, and the ratio p(prompt | θ)/p(prompt | θ*) is exp(n r_n(θ)); under **distinguishability**, for every θ ≠ θ* the sum over the k positions of the expected KL between the prompt's and θ's next-token distributions exceeds two mismatch terms (one from the prompt start distribution, one from the delimiter transitions), r_n(θ) has a negative limit and the predictor reaches the optimal 0-1 risk as n grows (Theorem 1; the Bernstein–von Mises route under dependence and mismatch, the delimiters used to factorise the examples with a constant error each). (b) Without distinguishability, if the KL has a second-order expansion around θ*, the excess risk is bounded by a term in (mismatch)/(k−1) scaled by the Fisher information's worst condition number (Theorem 2), and the same order holds with test examples of random length without continuity (Theorem 3): information in the inputs, not only in the input–output mapping, drives in-context learning, and longer examples help. (c) The assumptions include **well-specification**: the prompt concept lies in the family Θ (Assumption 4); extrapolation to unseen concepts is named as future work. (d) GINC, a synthetic corpus from a factorial HMM (entities that persist with probability at least 0.9 times properties; the concept is the property transition matrix; five concepts; vocabulary 50 to 150): GPT-2-style transformers of 4, 12 and 16 layers and LSTMs all learn in-context; accuracy rises with the number of examples, with the example length and with model size even at the same pretraining loss; one concept only, or random transitions, gives no in-context learning; prompts from **unseen concepts fail** (their Fig. 3, right); order sensitivity and cases where zero-shot beats few-shot are reproduced; LSTMs beat transformers on GINC (§4).

**Placement of our problem against theirs.** Their concept is our table; their mixture over documents is our corpus at radius zero with one table per window (the switch rate set so); their posterior concentration in n is our readers' curve by rank. What they assume away is exactly our axis: the prompt concept must lie in the training family (c), and in their simulation a concept outside it fails flat. Our drift moves the window's table out of the training ball by a graded distance, so the concept problem here is their setting with Assumption 4 dropped and the concept displaced by a bounded walk; Izzy's prediction (graded recovery in the distance) and the sharp alternative are two readings of the region their theorem leaves open and their experiment samples at one far point. Their distinguishability condition is the shape of a bound in our terms: the per-position KL between two tables' laws is a function of their distance (the delta), against mismatch terms set by the window's packing (our separator is their delimiter; `corpus.pack_window`), and the Fisher-information form of Theorem 2 is a candidate form for the excess-over-the-floor bound in k the general model is after (docs/proofs/general-model.md, the conjecture at its end). **Replication:** the radius-zero, one-table-per-window corpus through our model is their mixture; the far-bin readings past the training radius are the experiment they did not run. **Status:** verified; the placement lodged for the proof document.

## 5. Chan, Santoro, Lampinen, Wang, Singh, Richemond, McClelland, Hill (2022), "Data Distributional Properties Drive Emergent In-Context Learning in Transformers", arXiv 2205.05055 (NeurIPS 2022)

**Read in full 2026-10-11 (the TeX source); verified.** **Their setting (§2):** sequences of Omniglot image–label pairs, eight pairs of context then a query image, labels fixed across training as in supervised data; a 12-layer transformer (embedding 64, 8 heads) over a ResNet image embedder, cross-entropy on the query's label; in-context learning measured on holdout classes with the labels reassigned per sequence (4-shot 2-way), in-weights learning on sequences of unique trained classes with their trained labels. **Their claims:** (a) in-context learning emerges only with *both* burstiness (the query's class recurring three times within the context, a second class too) and a large number of rarely occurring classes (100 to 1600 to 12 800); either alone fails (§3.1); (b) dynamic meanings, several labels per class or within-class variation, increase it; (c) it trades off against in-weights learning in every sweep, and a model with an early in-context bias can lose it with further training (§3.1, the seed of entry 7); (d) a Zipfian marginal over classes with exponent 1 is the one setting where both coexist; the uniform marginal gives the most in-context learning and no in-weights learning, stronger skew the reverse (§3.2); (e) RNNs and LSTMs matched for depth, width and parameters never show in-context learning on the same data (§3.3).

**What the memory entry had wrong.** The Zipfian skew is not a driver of emergence but the condition for coexistence; the drivers are burstiness and the number of rare classes.

**For our data.** Burstiness is several artifacts of one table inside a window (the switch rate and the walk's persistence); many rare classes is the ensemble (41 000 distinct states over 42 000 artifacts, every state rare); dynamic meaning is the drift (one form, moved shares). Their result predicts the ensemble sits in the in-context regime, and that the in-weights predictor and the in-context reader trade off: the onset should cost the marginal copy's advantage where in-weights reading is measured (the near bins of the readings by distance), a reading available in 07e's lines. Their Zipfian knob we do not have: the forms' shares are the schema's, not a power law. **Replication:** none needed for the prediction; a sweep of the lean stays the test. **Status:** verified; read against 07e in round one.

## 6. Reddy (2024), "The mechanistic basis of data dependence and abrupt learning in an in-context classification task", arXiv 2312.03002 (ICLR 2024)

**Read in full 2026-10-11 (the TeX source); verified.** **Their setting (§2):** sequences of N = 8 item–label pairs then a target; items from a Gaussian mixture with K classes (each class a D = 63 vector, within-class noise ε), L = 32 labels, position a one-hot in a random window of P = 65 (translation-invariant by construction); at least one context item from the target's class; burstiness B (copies of a class per sequence), p_B the bursty fraction, a rank-frequency exponent α; a two-layer single-head attention-only network (residual, causal) then a three-layer MLP classifier; SGD at 0.01, batch 128, six seeds. Metrics: in-weights accuracy on sequences whose target class is absent from the context; in-context accuracy on novel classes with B copies of the target in the context (and a swapped-label variant).

**Their claims.** (a) Chan et al.'s dependencies (entry 5) are recapitulated in this minimal setting: K, B and ε raise in-context learning and lower in-weights learning; α = 1 gives both (the common classes in weights, the rare ones in context); the network converges to the in-weights solution whenever p_B < 1; both solutions can be learned at once (§3, App. A.1). (b) In-context accuracy shows a slow phase then an abrupt transition to perfect accuracy; before it both attention maps are unstructured, after it layer one attends to the previous token and the target attends to its correct label (§3, their Fig. 3). Four progress measures: ILA1 (previous-token attention in layer one), TILA2 (the target's attention to the correct label in layer two), CLA (the probability that the prediction is a label present in the context), TLA2 (the target's total attention to the labels). The slow phase is CLA and TLA2 rising, the network learning to pick one of the N context labels at random (accuracy 1/L to 1/N); the transition is ILA1 and TILA2 appearing together, an induction head. (c) A three-parameter model of the induction head (β1 the previous-token attention, α the content-to-buffer match, β2 the attention to labels) plus the classifier reproduces the measures; with β2 fixed at zero the transition is unchanged, so the label attention is not necessary (their H1 and H2 ruled out), and the two-parameter model reproduces every data dependence (App. A.3). (d) A phenomenological loss L = log(1 + (N−1)e^{−z} + (L−N)e^{−z'}), three nested logits (β1, α, ξ: the first attention, the second attention, the classifier), learned sequentially under gradient descent: ξ rises slowly from the origin (gradient (L−N)/(L(2N+1))) until the loss falls off a cliff and α and β1 are learned at once; an "intrinsic curriculum" (§3). (e) With the slow phase ablated (L = N, B = 1) in-context learning is fragile: two of six seeds reach it, four converge to a sub-optimal minimum; the landscape has a saddle at the origin whose two sides lead to the two outcomes (App. A.4, A.5). (f) In-context learning is not transient in their simulations, against entry 7, which they attribute to their smaller network and unlimited data (§4).

**What the memory entry had wrong.** The "few parameters" are three nested logits, two for the induction head and one for the classifier, not "the number of classes, burstiness, within-class variation", which are the data's parameters; the abruptness is placed on the nested nonlinearities' cliffs, and the transition's position on the slow phase's duration.

**For our data.** Their progress measures are our circuit scores (ILA1 is the previous-token score, TILA2 the induction score; `cocoonml/circuits.py`); CLA has a direct analogue we lack, the probability mass the model puts on the forms present in the window so far, one reader. Their claim that the transition's timing is set by a slow sub-optimal in-context strategy, and that without such a strategy the outcome splits by seed at a saddle, is a candidate account of decision (d) at (192, 9) radius 1 (the escape not monotone in the rate: the seed axis), to be tested when the seed row runs: if our marginal plateau carries no slow in-context phase (no CLA-like rise before the onset), their model predicts seed-dependence of the kind 07e showed. Methodology to take: the hypothesis test by ablation (a parameter fixed at zero; the slow phase removed) with the progress measures aligned at the transition across seeds. **Replication:** their task is pairs with labels, not a generator's strings; the methodology transfers, the task does not; the CLA analogue and the aligned measures on the 07e copies are the replication. **Status:** verified; the reader proposed; read against 07e in round one.

## 7. Singh, Chan, Moskovitz, Grant, Saxe, Hill (2023), "The Transient Nature of Emergent In-Context Learning in Transformers", arXiv 2311.08360 (NeurIPS 2023)

**Read in full 2026-10-11 (the TeX source); verified.** **Their setting (§2):** Chan et al.'s Omniglot sequences with every training sequence bursty (three pairs of the query's class, three of a distractor) and the labels fixed, so in-context learning alone suffices for the training loss; 12 layers, embedding 64, sinusoidal positions, Adam with warm-up and inverse-square-root decay, up to 5e7 steps, two seeds; also exemplars made from clustered LLaMa token embeddings.

**Their claims.** (a) In-context learning emerges, peaks and decays to chance while in-weights learning rises and the training loss keeps falling (§3), although in-context learning suffices; Chan et al. had stopped at 5e5 steps, before the peak. (b) Transience holds across depths (no trend in peak or onset, similar decay slopes), dataset sizes (more classes: higher peak and shallower decay; more exemplars per class: higher peak but steeper decay; at a fixed pair count more classes beat more variation) and the token-embedding datasets (§4); a Zipfian marginal at exponent 1 delays both the onset and the decay, exponent 2 prevents emergence (§4.4). (c) L2 regularisation up to about 1e-4 removes the decay on the tested timescales and makes in-weights learning transient instead, so the in-context circuit is the lower-norm solution; stronger regularisation brings transience back with no in-weights learning at all (§5). (d) The proposed cause is competition for the residual stream: wider embeddings raise the peak and flatten the decay without raising in-context learning on data that only in-context learning can solve; weight decay on the MLP or ResNet layers mitigates transience, on the attention layers it does not, in line with in-weights information living in the MLPs (§6, App. E).

**For our data.** Decision 53 runs every cell to its cap with no stopping rule and keeps the best and the last copy, so transience is readable as a best step well before the last with the held loss rising after. Our model is attention-only with no MLP, and their account places the in-weights circuit in the MLPs, so their mechanism predicts little or no transience in ours; a held loss rising after the best in a 07e cell would be a transience their account does not cover (the readout and the values its only home). Methodology to take: the controls trained on data only one strategy can solve, to separate capacity from competition; weight decay per block as an ablation. **Replication:** none needed; the best-against-last reading on 07e's twenty-four lines in round one. **Status:** verified; read after the record.

## 8. Bietti, Cabannes, Bouchacourt, Jégou, Bottou (2023), "Birth of a Transformer: A Memory Viewpoint", arXiv 2306.00802 (NeurIPS 2023)

**Read in full 2026-10-11 (the TeX source); verified; the memory entry's outline holds and gains the detail below.** **Their setting (§3):** a bigram model (a Markov chain) with the global unigram and bigram statistics of character-level tiny Shakespeare (vocabulary 65), where in each sequence K trigger tokens (fixed, or drawn per sequence) are always followed by a sequence-specific output token drawn uniformly; length 256, batches of fresh sequences, SGD with momentum and weight decay. **Their model (§4.2):** a two-layer single-head transformer simplified for identifiability: embeddings, positions, the first layer's value and output matrices and the second layer's value matrix frozen at random initialisation; W_Q = I so one key matrix per layer; trained W_K^1, W_K^2, W_O^2 and a linear feed-forward W_F; a one-layer model reaches about 55% on the output tokens against 99% (fixed triggers) and 95% (random triggers) for two layers.

**Their claims.** (a) With near-orthogonal random embeddings every weight matrix is an associative memory, a sum of outer products of input and output embeddings (eq. 4.1), and the gradient of the cross-entropy with fixed embeddings is exactly such a sum weighted by the prediction error (Lemma 4.1); with superposed inputs (residual sums) the gradient filters the noise and keeps the conditional means (Lemma 5.1). (b) The induction head is three memories: W_K^1 maps each position to the previous one, W_K^2 maps a trigger's embedding to its remapped copy from layer one, W_O^2 maps the remapped output token to its output embedding; the global bigrams live in W_F as log π_b (eqs. 4.3, 4.4). (c) Memory recall probes (the argmax of each matrix against its target memory) show the order of learning: W_O^2 is learnable first and alone, with uniform attention, as a bag of words; the key matrices learn nothing until it has; then W_K^2, then W_K^1; a theorem gives the same order as three sequential population gradient steps from random initialisation (§5, App. B). (d) Trained jointly, the global bigram statistics are learned first (the quick drop of the loss and of the KL of W_F to π_b) and the in-context mechanism later, the key matrices being the slow part (§4, their Fig. 4); W_O^2 and W_F compete later in training. (e) Data effects: fewer triggers, rare fixed triggers, and random triggers slow the in-context mechanism (the first two through W_O^2, the third through W_K^2); uniform output tokens generalise to bigram-sampled outputs, bigram-sampled outputs do not generalise to uniform ones. (f) The viewpoint extends to factorised key-query products, layer norm (which projects updates off the stored direction), MLPs (non-linear memories for sets) and several heads, where redundancy spreads a mechanism across heads (App. D, E).

**For our data.** The order "global statistics first, in-context mechanism later" is the marginal plateau then the onset, and their finer order is a prediction for the circuits reader by layer: the first layer's previous-token score is the last to rise, after the second layer's induction score and after the readout has formed (the best copy against the marginal copy, per layer, in `cocoonml/circuits.py`'s output). Their memory recall probe is a second reader we could build: our embeddings are trained, not frozen, but their App. E shows the probes still read with trained embeddings. Their Lemma 4.1 is the mechanism behind decision 41's reading of the initialisation off the counts: with embeddings of variance 1/d the updates are maximal-feature-learning updates, and a matrix's content after training is a weighted sum of embedding pairs. **Replication:** their task is one option away from entry 1's in `cocoonml/markov.py`: a global chain shared by every window plus K per-window trigger-to-output pairs; not run. **Status:** verified; the per-layer order lodged as a reading for round one on 07e's copies.

## 9. Makkuva, Bondaschi, Girish, Nagle, Jaggi, Kim, Gastpar (2024), "Attention with Markov: A Framework for Principled Analysis of Transformers via Markov Chains", arXiv 2402.04161 (ICLR 2025 as "A Curious Case of Single-Layer Transformers"); and Nichani, Damian, Lee (2024), "How Transformers Learn Causal Structure with Gradient Descent", arXiv 2402.14735

**Makkuva et al., read in full 2026-10-11 (the TeX source); verified; the memory version's error below.** **Their setting (§2, §3):** a single-layer transformer (one head, ReLU feed-forward, no layer norm, trainable positions) on a binary first-order Markov chain with kernel P(p, q), the switching factor p + q; the loss the next-token cross-entropy over positions, which equals the KL to the kernel plus the entropy rate (Lemma 1). **Their claims.** (a) The bigram (the kernel) is a global minimum with loss the entropy rate H(x_{n+1} | x_n), realised by ignoring the attention and reading x_n off the skip connection; trained models do that (the attention's contribution about 1% of the stream; constant positional embeddings; rank-one weights; the predicted probabilities match p and 1−q) (Theorem 1, App. E). (b) With **weight tying** (the readout equal to the embedding) and p + q > 1 there is a **bad local minimum** at the unigram: readout zero, bias log(p/q), prediction the stationary π, loss H(π), gap to the optimum exactly the mutual information I(x_n; x_{n+1}); the Hessian's non-zero block is positive-definite by the term 2(p+q−1)I (Theorem 2). (c) Without weight tying the same point is a saddle (Theorem 3), and empirically the untied model always reaches the global minimum; with tying and p + q > 1 it stays at the unigram; for p + q < 1 every run reaches the bigram either way (their Figs. 4, 5); five-state chains behave alike with the threshold above 0.5 (§4.3). (d) Models of depth two and more reach the global minimum regardless, **passing first through a plateau at H(π)** and dropping after (§4.4); their earlier claim that higher-order chains could not be learned is withdrawn (training was too short). A companion paper, "Local to global" (not downloaded), shows local minima also for p + q < 1 whose basin the Gaussian initialisation falls outside.

**What the memory entry had wrong.** The conditions for the unigram trap are the switching factor and weight tying, not "initialisation and the chain's switching"; initialisation is the companion paper's subject.

**For our data.** Their chain is fixed across sequences, so the bigram is an in-weights solution and the plateau at H(π) followed by a drop happens with **no in-context learning at all**: a marginal plateau then an onset is not by itself evidence of in-context reading. The control this gives the apparatus: a one-table corpus (one origin, radius zero, no switching) through our model, where the plateau and the drop are in-weights by construction, read with the same readers; any onset in the multi-table cells is read against it. Our model does not tie the readout to the embedding (decisions 41 lists the readout as its own block), so their saddle, not their minimum, is the point our marginal plateau sits at. Their gap formula is an analytical check for sweep 08's lines: the marginal level minus the floor at steps zero should approach the mutual information between consecutive tokens averaged over the chains (the floor reaches the entropy rate as windows grow). **Replication:** the one-table control cell, not run. **Status:** Makkuva verified; the control proposed.

**Nichani, Damian, Lee (2024), from memory, unverified (downloaded, not yet read):** gradient descent on a two-layer transformer trained on in-context Markov chains learns the chain's causal graph in the first layer's attention. **For our data:** the derived initialisation of decision 41 is where an initialisation-dependent result would bear on ours. **Replication:** their one-layer Markov setting on our model at radius zero. **Status:** planned.
