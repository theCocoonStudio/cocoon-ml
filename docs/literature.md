## 1. Edelman, Edelman, Goel, Malach, Tsilivis (2024), "The Evolution of Statistical Induction Heads: In-Context Learning Markov Chains", arXiv 2402.11004 (v1, 2024-02)

**Read in full 2026-10-11 (the TeX source, research session); verified against the paper; the memory version's errors listed below.** Izzy's reference ("the paper describing circuits with Bayesian math", 2026-10-10 23:1x).

**Their setting (§2.1, App. B).** Each training sequence is drawn from its own Markov chain on k states (k = 2 or 3 in the main experiments), every row of the transition matrix drawn from Dir(α) with α = (1, ..., 1), the uniform prior on the simplex; x_1 from the chain's stationary distribution; sequence length t = 100; the loss the next-token cross-entropy averaged over positions. **Their model (§2.3):** attention-only transformers, two layers, causal mask, relative position embeddings, hidden dimension 16, AdamW at 3e-5, batch 64. **The two strategies (§2.2):** *unigram*, count the states seen so far and predict from the posterior over the stationary distribution (no closed form for finite k); *bigram*, count the transitions out of the current state and predict (c_ij + 1)/(N_i + k), the Dirichlet posterior mean, which is the Bayes-optimal predictor up to the first token.

**Their claims, as the paper states them.**
- (a) A two-layer attention-only transformer represents the bigram solution exactly (Prop. 2.1): layer one attends to the previous token and copies it into the stream; layer two at position i attends to every earlier position whose previous token equals x_i and copies the token found there; the readout is the count. Trained models converge near the Bayes-optimal loss with the attention patterns of that construction (§3.1): the *statistical induction head*.
- (b) Training passes through **three** stages: uniform predictions, then a plateau at the in-context unigram predictor, then a rapid drop to the bigram solution; the KL divergence between the model's prediction and each strategy, plotted over training, shows the handover (§3.1, their Fig. 1 right).
- (c) A one-layer transformer, with or without MLPs, with absolute or relative positions, never undergoes the transition and never reaches the solution, even trained eight times longer (App. D, their Fig. 12).
- (d) The unigram solution delays learning: on a prior where the unigram statistics carry no signal (a = b in the two-state parameterisation) the model skips the plateau and converges much faster, even when evaluated on the original distribution (§3.1, their Fig. 4; Cor. A.2: the cubic term of the gradient vanishes).
- (e) The mechanism, shown in a minimal linear model f(E) = mask(E W_k (M E)^T) E, W_k standing for the second layer and the relative-position vector v for the first: after one SGD step from a constant initialisation the second layer's gradient has a diagonal bias (B ≈ 4A, Lemma 3.1) while v's gradient carries nothing about the look-back-one coordinate and favours v_1 with a quadratic profile; a uniform W_k with v_1 dominant *is* the unigram solution; v_2 overtakes only at the second step, once W_k is aligned. "Alignment of layers": the first layer's learning signal is tied to how far the second layer has come. A two-step curriculum with η_1 = O(1/t^2), then η_2 = O(1/t) with weight decay, reaches the bigram solution (Prop. 3.3). The even coordinates of v grow by a geometric series in the chain's second eigenvalue, an even/odd pattern they also find in the transformers' positional attention just before the second drop.
- (f) Three-grams with three heads per layer show three plateaus (unigram, bigram, trigram); single-headed models cannot beat bigrams there (§3.3).

**What the memory entry had wrong.** Two stages, not three (the uniform stage was missing). "A saddle or a slower direction": the paper's account is the layers' alignment through the structure of the first-step gradient, not a saddle. The Bayes-optimal reference is closed-form for the bigram arm only; their unigram reference has no closed form. The circuit attends to the positions *after* the earlier matches, not to the matches (the same information; our induction score, `cocoonml/circuits.py`, reads the position after the previous occurrence, which is theirs).

**What it predicts for our data, corrected.** Their three stages are three predictors: the uniform (no information), the in-context unigram (the window's own state frequencies so far), the in-context bigram (the window's transitions). Our *marginal level* (decisions 54, `reference.token_counts`) is the training corpus's token frequencies, a global predictor: under their symmetric prior that is their **first** stage, not the unigram plateau. So our cells' plateau at the marginal level maps to their uniform stage, and their unigram plateau is a predictor our references do not carry at all. Repair, an instrument: the in-context unigram predictor as a **third reference** beside the marginal level and the floor, the smoothed counts of the window so far ((c_i + 1)/(n + k) in the Markov world; the window's form counts in the schema world), in `reference.py`, `runs/reference7.py` and `runs/sweep8.py`, so every trajectory is placed against all three and "does our model pass through their unigram stage" becomes a reading. Their KL-to-strategy curves are a reader we lack: the KL of the model's predictive from each reference, per window, over training, cheap beside the loss. Their (c) is what the one-layer control 07g lodges (entry 3). Their (d) bears on the ensemble: a corpus whose marginal carries signal lengthens the plateau; the schema world's marginal is non-uniform by construction (unequal shares), so a long plateau is expected there, and a corpus with the marginal flattened is the cheap test of (d) on our side.

**Our replication.** Sweep 08's two cells (decisions 55: the Markov world, 4 states, Dir(1), resolution 8, size (96, 7), rate 0.05, cap 2488; stationary, then drift at radius 1) ran 2026-10-11 05:03 to about 05:30; their lines are unread until round one. Differences from theirs to hold while reading: k = 4 against their 2 or 3 (their appendix: with more symbols the unigram phase needs longer sequences to be visible, 200 for them); our window length; our position handling (to state from `array_attention.py` when read); our loop (plain descent at one rate; theirs AdamW 3e-5); evaluation over all positions (theirs at the last token). **Status:** paper verified; replication run, unread; the third reference proposed, not built.

**The theory question it raises (for Izzy).** Whether our in-context optimum is computable: closed-form under their generator; computable under ours by the exact path with no steps and by a particle filter with steps (decisions 54, built on 2026-10-10 23:4x); and even in their model one reference, the unigram-optimal, has no closed form.

## 2. Olsson, Elhage, Nanda et al. (2022), "In-context learning and induction heads"

**Their claim**, from memory: induction heads, attention heads that find the previous occurrence of the current token and copy what followed, form during training in a phase change, and that phase change coincides with the sharp improvement of the model's in-context learning score (the loss at late positions against early ones); the evidence is correlational across model sizes and causal in small attention-only models. Their *induction score* (attention from a token to the position after its previous occurrence) and *previous-token score* are the two numbers the circuits reader computes. **For our data:** the onset should coincide with the induction score rising on the best copy. **Replication:** the two scores per copy on the held windows; their phase change against our onset step. **Status:** reader written (`cocoonml/circuits.py`), not yet run on a record.

## 3. Elhage, Nanda, Olsson et al. (2021), "A mathematical framework for transformer circuits"

**Their claim**, from memory: attention-only transformers decompose into paths through the residual stream; a two-layer model's induction circuit arises by composition between a layer-one previous-token head and a layer-two head (K-composition); one-layer attention-only models are bigram and skip-trigram statistics and cannot do induction. **For our data:** our model is attention-only with layers in a residual stream (`cocoonml/array_attention.py`), the same family, so their decomposition applies as written; a one-layer cell should not show an onset of the induction kind. **Replication:** a one-layer cell at the smallest size as the control for the circuits reader: `sweep7.py sweep-07g-r1-w96-l1-lr0.05 4 3 4 96 1 64 2 1 1 0.5 42000 0,1,2,3,free 2000 2488 1 0.05 3 300 3/4 cap <copies>`, the schema's world at (96, 1), below the derived floor on purpose (the control, not a cell of the row), the same rate, cap and ensemble as 07e's (96, 7) cells at radius 1; what it must show for their decomposition to apply: an induction score that does not rise on its best copy, and a held loss that stays nearer the marginal level than the seven-layer cell's. To run after 07e frees the box, beside sweep 08's first cells. **Status:** planned, the command written.

## 4. Xie, Raghunathan, Liang, Ma (2022), "An explanation of in-context learning as implicit Bayesian inference"

**Their claim**, from memory: when the pretraining distribution is a mixture of hidden Markov models, each document from one latent concept, a model that predicts well in-context is doing Bayesian inference over the latent concept from the prompt, and in-context learning emerges even when the prompt's format differs from pretraining. **For our data:** our tables are their concepts, our corpus a mixture over tables with the walk inside a radius as the structure their HMMs lack; the readers' far-bin curve by rank is the posterior sharpening with k in their terms. **Replication:** their mixture setting is our radius zero with tables switched per window; the comparison is the shape of the curve in k. **Status:** planned.

## 5. Chan, Santoro, Lampinen, Wang, Singh, Richemond, McClelland, Hill (2022), "Data Distributional Properties Drive Emergent In-Context Learning in Transformers", arXiv 2205.05055 (NeurIPS 2022)

**Read in full 2026-10-11 (the TeX source); verified.** **Their setting (§2):** sequences of Omniglot image–label pairs, eight pairs of context then a query image, labels fixed across training as in supervised data; a 12-layer transformer (embedding 64, 8 heads) over a ResNet image embedder, cross-entropy on the query's label; in-context learning measured on holdout classes with the labels reassigned per sequence (4-shot 2-way), in-weights learning on sequences of unique trained classes with their trained labels. **Their claims:** (a) in-context learning emerges only with *both* burstiness (the query's class recurring three times within the context, a second class too) and a large number of rarely occurring classes (100 to 1600 to 12 800); either alone fails (§3.1); (b) dynamic meanings, several labels per class or within-class variation, increase it; (c) it trades off against in-weights learning in every sweep, and a model with an early in-context bias can lose it with further training (§3.1, the seed of entry 7); (d) a Zipfian marginal over classes with exponent 1 is the one setting where both coexist; the uniform marginal gives the most in-context learning and no in-weights learning, stronger skew the reverse (§3.2); (e) RNNs and LSTMs matched for depth, width and parameters never show in-context learning on the same data (§3.3).

**What the memory entry had wrong.** The Zipfian skew is not a driver of emergence but the condition for coexistence; the drivers are burstiness and the number of rare classes.

**For our data.** Burstiness is several artifacts of one table inside a window (the switch rate and the walk's persistence); many rare classes is the ensemble (41 000 distinct states over 42 000 artifacts, every state rare); dynamic meaning is the drift (one form, moved shares). Their result predicts the ensemble sits in the in-context regime, and that the in-weights predictor and the in-context reader trade off: the onset should cost the marginal copy's advantage where in-weights reading is measured (the near bins of the readings by distance), a reading available in 07e's lines. Their Zipfian knob we do not have: the forms' shares are the schema's, not a power law. **Replication:** none needed for the prediction; a sweep of the lean stays the test. **Status:** verified; read against 07e in round one.

## 6. Reddy (2024), "The mechanistic basis of data dependence and abrupt learning in an in-context classification task"

**Their claim**, from memory: in a minimal in-context classification task the abrupt emergence of in-context learning is the formation of an induction head, and a phenomenological model of a few parameters reproduces the transition and its dependence on the data's properties (the number of classes, burstiness, within-class variation). **For our data:** the onset's abruptness and its position against the crossing (decision 53, prediction (3)) are what their model is about; if their reduced model fits ours, the crossing is not the clock, their parameters are. **Replication:** fit their phenomenological model to our held trajectories. **Status:** planned after 07e.

## 7. Singh, Chan, Moskovitz et al. (2023), "The transient nature of emergent in-context learning in transformers"

**Their claim**, from memory: in-context learning that emerges during training can fade with further training as in-weights learning takes over, when the data allows both. **For our data:** decision 53 runs every cell to its cap with no stopping rule and keeps the best and the last copy; a best copy well before the last, with the held loss rising after, is their transience; the second descent is the opposite movement. **Replication:** none needed; read on the 07e trajectories (best step against last step). **Status:** read after 07e.

## 8. Bietti, Cabannes, Bouchacourt, Jégou, Bottou (2023), "Birth of a transformer: a memory viewpoint"

**Their claim**, from memory: on a synthetic bigram task where some bigrams are fixed and others vary per sequence, a two-layer model learns the global (in-weights) bigram statistics first and the in-context induction mechanism later; the weights are read as associative memories and the dynamics explained as the memories forming in order. **For our data:** the order "global statistics first, in-context mechanism later" is the marginal plateau then the onset; their associative-memory reading of the weight matrices is a second circuits reader we could build. **Replication:** their task is a Markov-chain corpus with a fixed part, one option away from entry 1's. **Status:** planned.

## 9. Nichani, Damian, Lee (2024), "How transformers learn causal structure with gradient descent"; Makkuva, Bondaschi et al. (2024), "Attention with Markov" and "Local to global: learning dynamics and effect of initialization for transformers"

**Their claims**, from memory: Nichani et al. prove that gradient descent on a two-layer transformer trained on in-context Markov chains learns the chain's causal graph in the first layer's attention; Makkuva et al. show that on first-order Markov data the loss landscape of a one-layer transformer has a local optimum at the unigram predictor whose basin depends on initialisation and the chain's switching, so training can stall at the unigram solution. **For our data:** the second is the marginal plateau as a local optimum, and its dependence on initialisation is the rate dependence the rounds found in the onset (decision 53, third form (2)); the derived initialisation of decision 41 is where their result bears on ours. **Replication:** their one-layer Markov setting on our model at radius zero. **Status:** planned.
