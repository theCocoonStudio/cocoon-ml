## 0. What round 1 left

- S = 1/Delta E (session 03, p. 93, written, not derived) inverts under the energy taken in round 1 (Delta E = T * (H(prior) - H(posterior)), the resolution achieved): the model that resolves more scores less, and 1/Delta E is undefined at zero resolution. So the asymptote cannot be a limit of energy; it is a limit of resolution at fixed weights as the distance grows, and S needs its own definition before that limit is restated. Its unit: in this theory every admissible number is a ratio of counts at a vantage, unitless; a number with a unit imports a vantage from outside; 1/Delta E carries one over energy, which changes with the unit of T. ## 2. Step 1, the direction

The legacy model computes M(w_t, x); the general model M(w_t, w_p, x) (session 03). As a direction: where the window's information lands, in the activations through fixed weights (legacy) or in the weights (general); the seam of 2026-09-16.

- Its failure condition is a rate, not emptiness (the owner, 2026-10-17: "isnt this a computability problem, really. model has to be big enough to absorb noise, and then it's a matter of if and when, not just if"): if the legacy model reaches the same resolution at a cost that does not grow with the distance, the direction says nothing. The literature's form of the objection is in-context learning as implicit gradient descent (von Oswald 2022, `docs/literature.md`); what answers it is a bound on the cost at which a fixed w_t reaches a resolution from x, hole 5.

## 3. Step 2, the goal formalized according to the direction

- **D0. S, the unresolved share (kept by the owner, 2026-10-18).** S(R, k, D) := ex_k(R | D) / ex_k(R0 | D), the reader's per-rank excess over windows at distance in D as a share of the marginal reader's; `general-model.md` 6.4 is the definition with its remarks. It measures the bits R leaves unresolved at rank k as a share of the bits the window could resolve at that rank; 0 at the Bayes reader, 1 at the marginal, no unit, no vantage outside the law. The owner's two questions of 2026-10-17 answered by it: S is 1 minus a share, so the limit is a statement that can be true or false; the unit is none. The denominator is the marginal's excess rather than the reader's own first rank: not a choice, the two determine each other (6.4), only the name moves. The statement below is in S: the owner's prediction is a positive limit in k that grows with the distance; the degenerate case S = 1 at every k; the direction, the general class's best member at size n being w_p (D4'), its limit is S(w_p, k, D), the trainer's own gap as a share, falling with n, and the legacy reader's limit exceeds it by an amount that grows with the distance.
- **D1. Index and protocol.** At each index t a protocol P_t, a distribution over pairs of form and meaning; the drift moves the index. (`general-model.md`, Parts I to III.)
- **D2. Distance.** d(t, t'), a ratio of counts in [0, 1] between two indices. Which count: hole 2.
- **D3. Window and model.** A window: k pairs drawn at index t' and one query form. A model is a map from a window to a distribution over V (the output map, round 1).
- **D4. The world's weights.** W(t): the weights training on P_t produces at size n. w_t = W(t), w_p = W(t'). A function of the index alone; no sample is an argument of it.
- **D4'. The two classes, by where the window's information lands.** L computes f(W(t); window). G computes f(G_hat; window), where G_hat is an estimate of W(t') from W(t) and the window alone, no oracle; the estimate's error is the residual (the page; `fixed-index-bound.md` D5), the part of W(t') that no window of length k identifies. The residual bounds every member of G and of L alike. L is the member of G whose estimate is W(t) itself, so every statement below is about the best member of each class at size n, for every n above some floor (the owner's "big enough to absorb noise").
- **D5. Resolution.** R(M, t', k) := 1 - S(M, k, D) with D the band at t' (D0); the count is the bits of 6.4, hole 3 closed.
- **D6. Cost.** k_M(r, d): the least k at which R(M, t', k) reaches r at every index t' at distance d from t; infinite if none does. This is `general-model.md` 6.1's recovery read the other way: rec gives the resolution at k, k_M gives the k at a resolution.
- **The statement.** For r below the identifiable share at k (the residual's complement), the function d to k_L(r, d) against d to k_G(r, d). The asymptote as the proof meant it: a d* beyond which k_L(r, d) is infinite while k_G(r, d) is finite everywhere. The owner's prediction (2026-10-08, 2026-10-09; `general-model.md` 6.2): k_L finite everywhere, growing with d, the growth smaller the more drift the training carried. One function, two shapes; the r_train row measures it at three values of the training drift. The direction's content is the difference between the two costs above the identifiability floor, which is the same floor for both classes.
- **What this adds to `general-model.md`.** Part V defines readers given the law and the trained reader; none whose parameter depends on the window. One definition to add, 4.5: a reader whose parameter is a function of the window (the class G); Part VII's statement is then about two readers.

## 4. Step 3, the first lemma that is new: the moving index (for the owner's strike)

`fixed-index-bound.md` Lemma 1 gives the sampling floor at a fixed index (derivation). Its section 7 left the index moving inside the window to the owner; the owner asked on 2026-10-17 for the steps to continue as they unfold.

**Lemma 4 (the moving index).** Let the window hold k artifacts drawn while the index moves, and let the share at node v move by at most L_v per step, s steps across the window. The frequency at v estimates a target that has moved by at most s * L_v, so its bias is at most s * L_v while its sampling error is about 1 / (2 * sqrt(n_v)); the share is resolved to about min(rho, sqrt(n_v), 1 / (s * L_v)) levels. If the index steps once per artifact, s grows with k while n_v = k * pi_v grows with k, so the resolution is largest at a finite k, of order (pi_v / L_v^2)^(1/3), and no k removes the floor. **Status:** derivation, first order, the bias taken as the range of the walk; the mean-reverting walk bounds the range by its radius whatever s, which caps the loss at large k. **Disproof:** a reader that pins a share finer than 1 / (s * L_v) from a window over which the share moved that much. **What it gives the statement:** a finite asymptote of recovery that is a property of the world and the window, before any reader is named; the first number in the bound B of `general-model.md` 6.3 that is not the sampling floor.

## 5. Prompt-time tasks, measured (the owner's questions, 2026-10-17)

- **Success at prompt time, and its tie to training.** In this world it is the same quantity read against a different law: the loss of 4.2, at prompt time the excess of that loss over the floor the reading law sets (4.4), recovery its fall with k (6.1). The tie is exact because the law is ours; training enters only through what the training law shares with the reading law, the identified part (6.3). For an English task the tie is hole 3: the threshold is empirical and nobody holds the law.
- **Orthogonal task, quantitative evaluation.** The task is orthogonal by the distance of its index; its evaluation is tied to the world at that index, not to training. The world's truth must exist and never be an input (the design of 2026-10-08).
- **Math and tokens.** Two seams, at training and reading alike: the readout (a distribution over V against the law; inside, integers) and the packing (forms to tokens, k strings with a separator, 3.1 and 3.2). The choices sit at the seams, not inside: the leaf forms with the empty form, the separator, the alphabet's cardinality. English adds the tokenizer outside the model, the orthography object.
- **"The model found a problem in the task."** The fixed readout has no slot for it. To account for it the output space must contain a statement about the task and the evaluation must hold the truth about the task. In this world both exist: a window under drift is inconsistent with every single table because the index moved inside it; that movement is the world's truth (`corpus.window_truth_distances`), and a model that reports it is scored against it like any output. Marked as Claude's: that second readout is G's estimate of the index, read out instead of hidden; "found a problem in the task" is the general class's defining output, and reading it is how the class becomes measurable.

## 6. The slope and the tasks (the owner's posit, 2026-10-17)

The owner: "if you focus on the wrong tasks, you cant bring out the slope"; "the tasks have to be varied enough"; quantify it "with task variables, and unknown out of scope variables". A slope is a relation among tasks ordered on the axis the prediction bends on (distance from the training index), with the evaluation held fixed across them; a point has no slope (the level-up: shuffle the tasks and the slope is gone). Quantified as the statistical layer already has it (decisions 50 and 53): the levels on the axis and the windows per level, against the null's envelope; "varied enough" is the ratio of the spread across levels to the spread within a level, a count over a count; a slope needs two levels, a bend three, each level's band narrower than the step. The unknown variables are not listed; they are the part of the within-level spread the known variables do not account for, measured by the envelope since the null is computed from the readings. The training side: the model tracks a variable only if its training varied it (the r_train row's reason), and the training's spread on the axis is set against the reading's. In the framework's words: the task set has a rank from the reader's vantage, and "enough" is the resolution along the axis.

## 7. Step 3, continued: the identified part of the two samples, and the prior's pull (draft, 2026-10-18, for strikes)

Written alone while the owner was out ("let's make some progress on it"), in the order section 8 names: after the floor at a fixed index (`fixed-index-bound.md` Lemma 1) and the moving index (Lemma 4), the identified part of the two samples, then the two classes compared above the floor. Everything used is defined before it: the ball and the distance (`general-model.md` 1.4, 2.4), the step kernel's radius and pull (1.2), the stationary law of the walk (1.3.1), the sampling floor (Lemma 1), the consistent mass m_v(x) and the sensitivity (`fixed-index-bound.md` D6, Lemma 3), S (6.4).

- **D7. The identified part.** For a reading table T' (the table the production holds at the reading index) and the training ball B_t, the distance of node v is delta_v(T') := the minimum over tables T in B_t of |T'(v) - T(v)|, grains over the grid's levels. The identified part of T' is the set of nodes with delta_v(T') = 0: the shares the training production visited. d(T', B_t) of 1.4 is at most the mean over nodes of delta_v, with equality when one training table attains every node's minimum.
- **D8. The training prior.** The stationary law pi_{C_t} of the training walk (1.3.1), read at node v as a distribution over the share's level: the training's prior at v. Its spread sigma_v is the standard deviation of that level under pi_{C_t}, in share units. For the kernel of 1.2 the pull is weak within rho_t of the centre and certain beyond it, so sigma_v is of the order of rho_t (status: the mean-reverting walk's stationary spread is its radius; the constant for this kernel is owed; `corpus.Ball` reads the spread from the record).
- **D9. The two ideal readers.** L_B, the Bayesian legacy reader: the Bayes reader under the training law W_t (4.3), read on a window from W_p; it conditions on the window with the training's prior at every node, a fixed function of W(t) and the window. G_flat: the reader that estimates every node's share from the window's frequency with no prior (the member of G whose estimate is the frequency); up to the sampling floor and the moving index it is R* under the reading law. Each is the best member of its class in the sense of D4': what a fixed function of W(t) and the window can do when the function is the posterior, and what an estimate from the window can do with no prior.

**Lemma 5 (the prior's pull).** At node v with n_v observations in the window and a share s' at the reading index, L_B's estimate of the share is biased toward the training ball by about kappa_v * delta_v / (n_v + kappa_v), where kappa_v := 1 / sigma_v^2 is the prior's weight in observations (a prior of spread sigma counts as 1 / sigma^2 observations at the share's scale, the conjugate form), while G_flat's bias is zero; both carry the sampling error of Lemma 1, about 1 / (2 * sqrt(n_v)). **Status:** derivation under the conjugate approximation of pi_{C_t} at v (a Beta with its mean within rho_t of the ball's nearest share and weight kappa_v), first order in delta_v; where delta_v is not large against rho_t the bias is of the order of kappa_v * (delta_v + rho_t) / (n_v + kappa_v). **Disproof:** a Bayesian reader under W_t whose estimate at a node outside the ball lands within the sampling error of the window's frequency while n_v is well below kappa_v.

**Corollary (the shape of the legacy cost).** By Lemma 3 the prediction's extra total variation at a query x is at most the sum over the nodes on x's consistent derivations of m_v(x) * kappa_v * delta_v / ((n_v + kappa_v) * min(s_v, 1 - s_v)), with n_v = k * pi_v in expectation. Read as S: S(L_B, k, D) - S(G_flat, k, D) is of that order divided by ex_k(R0 | D). It falls with k as 1/k, rises with the distance through delta_v, and is smaller for a wider training walk, since kappa_v = 1 / sigma_v^2 falls as rho_t grows. The third is the owner's prediction of 2026-10-09 (`general-model.md` 6.2, "the growth smaller the more drift the training carried"), derived: a training that varied a share holds it with a weaker prior. The first is the cost k_L(r, d) of D6: finite at every d and growing with d, about kappa_v * delta_v / r at a target r above the floors. The limit in k of S(L_B, k, D) is S(G_flat, k, D)'s: the pull washes out.

**The fork, the next hole (hole 7; typed here, the owner's to strike).** Lemma 5 gives the legacy class no positive asymptote: an ideal Bayesian legacy reader converges, late. The owner's prediction of a positive limit that grows with the distance (6.2) is therefore not a statement about L_B. It is a statement about the trained reader w_t, whose in-context estimator is not the posterior but a function g the training objective fitted. The objective constrains g on windows drawn from W_t, whose tables lie in the ball; on a window from W_p at distance d, g is evaluated where no training window constrained it. Two cases, and the definitions above decide neither:

- (a) g extrapolates as the posterior does (the architecture implements the estimator): the limit is zero and the cost is Lemma 5's; the asymptote is a rate, and the direction's content is k_L against k_G.
- (b) g extrapolates otherwise: the gap at distance d is what the architecture does off the training support, bounded by nothing in the definitions; a positive limit growing with d is then a property of the reader class at size n, and its lower bound needs an assumption about that class (capacity, inductive bias, the form of its in-context estimator), which the general model does not have and the apparatus can only measure.

The r_train row (concept 03) measures which case holds: three training radii at one reading distance; under (a) the three curves differ in rate and share a limit, under (b) they differ in the limit. This is the proof's next decision, not its next lemma.

- **Correction from the literature, the same day (`docs/literature.md` entries 13, 16, 21; cocoon-ml #32).** Raventós et al. (2023) measure Lemma 5's two readers in linear regression: the Bayes estimator under the training prior and the flat (ridge) estimator. Their training prior is the uniform measure on the tasks the training saw, and a trained reader's prior is likewise the ball's empirical measure, not the walk's stationary law as D8 says. Below a threshold in the number of distinct tasks the trained reader tracks the Bayes estimator under that discrete prior, whose posterior converges to the nearest seen task, so its limit is positive and grows with the distance: the owner's prediction, from a fixed-weight reader. Above the threshold the trained reader leaves its own prior for the flat estimator, the limit zero. So the conjugate approximation of Lemma 5 (a smooth prior, the pull washing out) holds above the threshold only, and the fork's case (b) is the discrete-prior regime below it; kappa_v also grows with training length at a fixed ball (their claims c and d), and Panwar et al. (2023) show it moving during one training run (forgetting), so S is read at every copy of a run. Lin and Lee (2024) give the pull in closed form with a mixture prior, the shift (I + k delta C)^{-1}(w_m + k delta m) that Lemma 5 approximates, and predict an early ascent at small k when the nearest training table misleads, so rec(w_t, k, D) may fall before it rises. For the r_train row: place the three training radii to straddle a threshold in the ball's cardinality, hold the window length equal across cells (Wu et al. 2023, the context-length cost), and read past the first k.
- **What this adds to B (6.3).** Lemma 5 is the first term of "what the training law shares with the reading law": the identified part enters as delta_v = 0, where the pull is harmless; the rest through kappa_v * delta_v / (n_v + kappa_v). The sampling floor (Lemma 1) and the moving index (Lemma 4) are the same for both classes and cancel in the difference.
